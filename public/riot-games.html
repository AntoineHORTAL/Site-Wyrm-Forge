For Riot Games — product key review

What's actually in Wyrm Forge, right now

Wyrm Forge is a companion site and desktop overlay for League of Legends. This page is a detailed, feature-by-feature snapshot of the product for anyone reviewing our production API key request: what's exclusive to the site, what's exclusive to the app, what's shared, what's built but not live yet, what we're actively working on, what's designed but not started, and what's still just an idea.

Compliance capability

Every feature can be switched off centrally, immediately

We built a single rollout system that controls both the site and the app from one internal dashboard. It was originally built to launch new features safely, but it doubles as a kill switch: almost every feature on this page — including every individual block of the overlay listed under "Exclusive to the app" (the comeback tracker, the build assistant, spell damage, the live matchup panel, jungle pathing, the inhibitor and nexus turret timers, item set export, rune page apply, the community workshop, and so on) — has its own separate switch, added specifically so any one of them can be disabled without touching the others or shipping a new build.

  • No deploy needed — toggled from an admin dashboard, not a code change.
  • Fast to apply — the site checks for changes every 60 seconds; the app keeps a local cache and re-checks on its own schedule.
  • Accountable — disabling a feature requires typing a reason first; there's no silent toggle.
  • Granular, block by block — every overlay panel is its own independent switch, so we can turn off one specific block (say, just the build assistant, or just item set export) while every other panel keeps working normally.
Website only — wyrm-forge.com

Exclusive to the site

Runs in the browser. Nothing here touches the game client directly.

  • Public site & dashboardMarketing pages plus a signed-in player dashboard, both fully translated into French and English.
  • Player & match searchLook up any summoner by name and browse their recent match history, no app install required.
  • Match detail pagesA dedicated deep-dive page per match, with a full stat and matchup breakdown for that game (part of the wider MatchUp system — see "Shared" below).
  • Patch notes pageA page summarizing official Riot patch notes, generated with AI assistance and kept French-only by choice.
  • Free champion rotationShows this week's free-to-play champion rotation on the dashboard, refreshed automatically from Riot's data.
  • Subscription & billingPricing page, checkout, and card payments through Stripe. Upgrades and downgrades happen here; the app only reads the result. As a precaution, new subscriptions can be temporarily paused during external reviews like this one — existing subscribers are never affected, and this is itself controlled by the kill-switch system described above.
  • Ads on the free tierBanner ads shown only to free-tier ("Apprentice") players inside their dashboard, served through Google AdSense. Never on the public marketing pages, never for paying players.
  • Admin dashboardInternal-only tool, not visible to players: managing user accounts, editing patch notes content, and switching features on or off through the rollout system, organized into several tabs.
  • Subscription self-serviceA player can cancel their subscription or update their payment card directly from their dashboard, through Stripe's customer portal, without contacting us.
Desktop app only — Windows overlay

Exclusive to the app

Runs alongside the League client while a game is live. Every overlay feature we currently have is listed below.

Live overlay panels

  • In-game overlayThe base transparent window drawn over the League client, reading live stats, gold, items and cooldowns from Riot's Live Client Data API.
  • Objective timersCountdowns for Dragon, Baron Nashor, Rift Herald and Voidgrubs, shown either as badges at the top of the screen or as a scrolling timeline of upcoming objectives, at the player's choice. Kill times, and the dragon's element, come from the event feed of Riot's Live Client Data API. The next spawn is then computed on the PC from fixed spawn and respawn delays built into the app (for example 5:00 between two dragons, 6:00 for Baron). The block also shows the dragons each team has taken, and who holds the Baron or Elder buff with the time left. A champion who dies is greyed out, since the buff is lost on death; this also comes from the same API's player list. Fully automatic; each part can be turned off in the app's Overlay tab.
  • Gold differenceAn estimate of the gold gap between the two teams, and between the player and their lane opponent. Riot's Live Client Data API doesn't give other players' gold, so each player's gold is estimated as the shop price of the items they hold, the same items anyone can see on the in-game scoreboard. The player's own unspent gold is added, because the API exposes it for the local player only; the other nine players' unspent gold is unknown, so the figures lean in the player's favour. The lane opponent is the enemy in the same slot of the API's player list. While the player holds the scoreboard key (Tab by default, configurable), the gap for each lane is shown next to the scoreboard and the other blocks are hidden. The app watches only these keys through a standard Windows keyboard hook; other keys are ignored and nothing is recorded. Fully automatic, calculated on the PC.
  • Enemy summoner spell trackerThe five enemy champions with their two summoner spells, read once at the start of the game from the player list of Riot's Live Client Data API. Spell use is not detected automatically. When the player sees an enemy use a spell, they click its icon, and the app counts down that spell's base cooldown from a table built into the app. Summoner spell haste (runes, boots) is not taken into account, so the countdown is a guide, not an exact timer. Horizontal or vertical layout, movable.
  • Stats blockThe player's level, CS, CS per minute, gold per minute, KDA and vision score, each one optional. They come from Riot's Live Client Data API and match the in-game scoreboard; gold per minute uses the estimate described under "Gold difference". Each figure can optionally be compared, in green or red, with the lane opponent's, from the same API.
  • To-do listA short checklist of up to 5 items that the player writes in the app (for example "ward before 3:00"). The active list is shown in the overlay and can be ticked off during the game. Fully manual, stored only on the player's PC; no game data involved.
  • Comeback trackerReads gold, objective and kill differentials as the game evolves and shows whether a team is pulling ahead, falling behind, or the game is close.
  • Build assistantAn AI-assisted suggestion for the next item purchase, based on the current game state and gold available. Internally nicknamed "Chaleur de la Forge."
  • Spell damageThe damage of each of the player's own abilities (passive, Q, W, E, R) at their current rank and stats, shown just above the ability bar. Ability ranks and stats come from Riot's Live Client Data API; the damage values come from champion data bundled with the app. To place the panel, the app takes one screenshot of the bottom of the screen and looks for the player's health bar in it (or, failing that, their champion portrait). This only happens when no position is saved for the current screen resolution: the first game at that resolution, or after the player resets the block positions. The screenshot is analysed in memory on the player's PC and is never uploaded or saved, not even in a debug mode.
  • Live matchup panelThe in-game half of the MatchUp system: tips about your lane opponent shown while you're actually playing (see "Shared" below for the site half).
  • Sidebar navigationLets a player switch between all of the panels above without leaving the overlay.
  • Hide a block for this game, reset the layoutHovering an overlay block for a moment shows a small ✕; one click hides that block for the current game only, without changing the player's settings, and it comes back in the next game. This cross is the only clickable part added: the rest of the block still lets clicks through to the game. Hiding a block can never bring back one that is switched off by a setting or a remote switch. The app's Overlay tab adds "Show this game's hidden blocks" (in game only) and "Reset block positions", which moves every block back to its default place without changing which blocks are shown. Shipped in version 1.0.16.

Jungle & minimap

  • Jungle pathing on the minimapA suggested jungle route drawn directly on the in-game minimap, with the list of camps in a panel placed just above it, which can be moved like any other panel.
  • Automatic minimap calibrationReads the minimap size setting from the game's own settings file and the screen resolution from Windows, then takes a few screenshots of the minimap corner to find the minimap's exact edges, so the jungle path and the timers line up with the in-game map; the player only confirms the detected outline once per setup (resolution, minimap size, display mode). Screenshots are analysed in memory on the player's PC and are never uploaded. They are not saved either: only a debug mode reserved for development, switched on by hand on our own machines, writes them to disk. Since version 1.0.16, a "Recalibrate the minimap" button in the app's Overlay tab forgets the calibration for the current setup and offers a new one in game, and an "Adjust the minimap timers" option lets the player nudge the position and overall size of the timers if needed. The app never reads game memory. This capture step can be switched off remotely at any time.
  • Minimap click-throughThe overlay only captures mouse clicks on its own interactive panels. Everywhere else, and always over the minimap, clicks go straight to the game underneath, so camera moves, movement orders and pings on the map are never blocked. The minimap area follows the calibrated minimap rather than any drawing on top of it, and a panel dragged onto the minimap is refused. Validated in real games; shipped in version 1.0.16.
  • Inhibitor respawn timersA 5:00 countdown drawn over each destroyed inhibitor, for both teams, from the inhibitor events of Riot's Live Client Data API. Colours follow the player, like the in-game minimap: blue for their own team's inhibitors, red for the enemy's, turning yellow in the last 30 seconds. Positions were measured on a real minimap at a reference minimap size; at other sizes they rely on the calibration above. Text size adjustable from ×0.75 to ×2. Shipped in version 1.0.16, with its own player setting and remote switch.
  • Nexus turret respawn timersA 3:00 countdown for each destroyed nexus turret, both teams, same look and colours as the inhibitor timers. The API reports when a turret is destroyed but not when it comes back, so the timer runs on the fixed respawn delay. Each label sits on the fountain side of its nexus so the two never overlap, nor the inhibitor timers next to them. Shipped in version 1.0.16 behind its own switch, separate from the inhibitor timers — now switched on.

Builds, items & runes

  • Item set exportExports a build directly into League's own item-set format, so it shows up as a quick-buy option in the in-game shop.
  • Rune page applyApplies a chosen rune page straight into the League client with one click, instead of setting it up manually.
  • Community workshopBrowse builds and jungle paths shared publicly by other players, from inside the app.

After the game & app upkeep

  • Post-game summaryA tab of the app's main window. The player enters their Riot ID once, and the tab shows their most recent match, fetched from the Match-V5 API through our server: their own stats, the stats and items of all ten players, the timeline of towers, inhibitors and epic monsters, and a short note when they died a lot or not at all. Stats are not compared with rank averages. Once the tab has been opened, the app checks every 30 seconds for a newer match; our server caches the match list for 3 minutes and finished matches permanently, so these checks do not each reach Riot.
  • Automatic updatesThe app checks for and installs new versions itself on launch. Old installer files are cleaned up automatically in the background to avoid piling up disk space.
Both surfaces

Shared between site and app

Systems that exist once but are read, shown, or applied on both surfaces.

  • Staged rollout & kill switchesSee the callout at the top of this page — this is the system behind it.
  • MatchUpOne matchup system with two faces: a detailed breakdown on the site's match pages, and a live version shown in the app's overlay during the game itself.
  • Subscription tiersWhat a player pays for on the site determines what they get in the app — one account, one entitlement, checked by both surfaces automatically.
  • Bilingual interfaceBoth the site and the app ship in French and English from the same hand-maintained translation files. A few things are deliberately left French-only, like League rank names.
Coded and tested, switched off

Built, not live yet

Finished, but kept behind the rollout system above so nothing new appears until it's ready for everyone at once.

  • Launch featuresSeasonal challenges and daily quests are coded and tested, waiting for the switch. An in-app currency ("Écailles") is also built, but we're still refining what it can be meaningfully spent on to make sure it adds real cosmetic value rather than filler — it isn't exchangeable for real money and isn't tied to any tournament or wagering mechanic.
  • Live game lookup (site and app)Shows the ten players of a game in progress with their ranks: on the site, on a public page that also shows each player's win rate; in the app, in a "Live game" tab of the main window (not the overlay). The ranks — and, on the site, the lineup itself — come from the Spectator-V5 and League-V4 APIs through our server: cached for 5 minutes, one Spectator call shared by all ten players, never refreshed automatically. Fully built and tested, but switched off with its own kill switch until our production API key is approved, to stay within the limits of our personal development key. Until then, the site shows a "coming soon" message, and the app shows the lineup it reads locally from the Live Client Data API, with "Ranks coming soon" in place of ranks.
  • Feature suggestion box & monthly voteLogged-in players will be able to submit a detailed feature request (title, description, category) from a new dashboard tab. Submissions stay private, visible only to their author, until a monthly voting round opens — at which point a shortlisted set becomes visible for players to vote on, with the author's identity never revealed even during voting. Fully built and tested, including an admin tool to open and close voting rounds, but held behind its own launch switch: it introduces a new kind of personal data (submitted ideas and votes tied to an account), so it's waiting on a privacy-policy update covering that before it opens.
  • Personal coaching packs (first phase)A player can submit a request for a tailored champion/role kit, which we then track internally as a case file. Booking is currently just an external link, and the kit itself is still put together and delivered by hand — the request submission and internal tracking are what's actually coded and held behind a launch switch. The live in-game coaching session is a separate, later step, still in the "thought through, not started" stage below.
Being worked on right now

In progress

Active work this month, mostly compliance-driven groundwork ahead of the features below.

  • Required billing emailsTransactional emails are being wired back in to cover three obligations under French consumer law: order confirmation, cancellation confirmation, and a reminder before an annual subscription renews.
  • Automatic structure positions on the minimapInstead of fixed positions, a few extra screenshots of the minimap corner early in the game (between 1:00 and 1:30) would locate the inhibitor and nexus icons, so the timers above sit exactly on them at any minimap size. Whether a structure is up or down always comes from Riot's Live Client Data API, never from the image: the pixels are only used to position the timers. Same rules as the calibration: analysed on the PC, never uploaded, never saved outside the development debug mode. Coded and tested on real minimap captures; shipped in version 1.0.16 but switched off by its own launch switch until it has been validated in real games.
  • Code signing certificate
    The app's installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows currently shows an "unknown publisher" SmartScreen warning on first install. An OV code-signing certificate has been ordered and is pending identity validation with the certificate authority; once issued, it will be wired into the release pipeline so every future build is signed automatically. Proof of this order is included directly below, since this application doesn't support file attachments.
Designed, no code yet

Thought through, not started

The mechanics, pricing, and edge cases are worked out; nothing has been built.

  • Personal coaching packs (live session)The final step of the coaching flow: 1-2 games played with a coach watching live to put the kit into practice. The request-submission and internal tracking steps are already built (see "Built, not live yet" above); booking and kit delivery are still manual, and this live-session step is designed but not coded.
  • Clans & guildsA free tier open to everyone and larger paid tiers for bigger groups, each with a ranking based on members' win rate, a search page, join requests reviewed by the group's leader, and a moderation process for reported groups. Fully specified, not coded.
  • "Legion" planA subscription shared by a group of five accounts, with one paying account inviting the other four. The structure is designed; today it exists only as a placeholder value in the database, with no real functionality behind it yet.
Discussed, not fleshed out

Just an idea

Come up in conversation, with no design work done and no timeline.

  • TournamentsAn earlier design (daily-to-monthly-to-annual, cash prizes) was scrapped after legal review found it wasn't viable under French law. We're starting completely over, much further down the line — nothing designed yet, and any future version would need to be rethought from scratch.
  • Match outcome predictionAn estimate of a game's odds while it's in progress. Mentioned as a possible future feature, no design done.
  • Video / replay analysisReviewing a recorded game rather than a live one. Mentioned as a "someday" feature; not designed or scheduled.
  • Windowed-mode support in the appThe overlay currently assumes the game always fills the whole primary screen. Supporting a windowed game client would need real design work; identified as a gap, not yet planned.
  • Beyond League of LegendsA long-term idea to support other Riot titles, starting with Teamfight Tactics, then possibly other games entirely. No timeline attached.
